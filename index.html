    A quick guide

    1. Build your lesson

    Add the lesson details and up to three objectives. Choose Add activity, then add questions, pictures, or both. Use the agenda’s up/down buttons to change the order. Set a duration for every slide.

    2. Teach from presentation view

    Choose Present lesson. Use Next, Previous, or Agenda to move through the lesson. Answers start hidden. Reveal next answer reveals just one; use Hide beside an answer or Hide all to cover it again. Pictures enlarge when clicked.

    The timer belongs to each slide. Start, Pause, and Reset work in both preview and presentation. Moving to another slide pauses the previous timer. Presentation starts with fresh timers and hidden answers. Use the arrow keys to move; press Esc or Exit to leave. If your browser does not allow full-screen, the presentation still fills the browser window.

    3. Save and back up

    Save lesson adds a new lesson to your library; later saves update that same lesson. Use Save a copy in My lessons to keep a separate version. Your working draft saves automatically, but the library changes only when you press Save lesson.

    Back up current downloads the open lesson. Back up library downloads all saved lessons plus the current draft and your preferences. Import adds copies, so existing lessons are never replaced. Images are included inside the backup.

    Local drafts and optional OneDrive snapshots. Connect OneDrive in the top toolbar and press Save to OneDrive to upload a lesson with its pictures and form links. A new cloud snapshot is created each time. Automatic draft saving still uses this browser. Student answers are collected in Microsoft Forms and its linked Excel workbook. Data belongs to this browser and device. Private browsing, clearing site data, changing browsers, moving this HTML file, or switching to a hosted address can make your library unavailable. Back up before any of these changes.

    Images and storage limits

    PNG, JPEG, WebP, and GIF pictures up to 8 MB each are accepted. Pictures over 1600 pixels on their longest side are reduced to save space; GIFs become still images. Pictures use “fit” display without cropping. Browser storage capacity varies. If a save fails, download a backup immediately and remove unused lessons or images. Backups are plain JSON files; keep them safe if they contain student information.

    Open and publish

    Double-click index.html to open it in a modern browser. Local lesson editing needs no account or installation. OneDrive saving and online student responses require internet, Microsoft sign-in, and the one-time connection setup. When you are ready, this same file can be published with GitHub Pages. Publishing the website does not publish your locally saved lessons. Import a backup at the new address, then connect OneDrive. Keep redirect.html next to index.html when publishing.

    Student responses & results

    Students submit through Microsoft Forms. Link a form to each activity in Edit activity; share its student response link with your class.

    Connect a form to your OneDrive results
    1. Sign in to Microsoft Forms with your work account and create your own personal form or quiz. Add the questions you want students to answer.
    2. Choose Collect responses. Select Specific people in my organization for your class where available, or Only people in my organization. Copy the response link.
    3. Paste that link into Student response link in Edit activity. Use Copy student link to share it with students.
    4. In Forms, open the responses area and choose Open in Excel. For your own work-account form, Microsoft saves the response workbook to your OneDrive. Keep the workbook private.
    5. Copy the workbook address from the browser’s address bar (or your Forms editing address). Paste it into My results link · teacher only. Use Open my results here to review responses.
    6. For quizzes, turn off Show results automatically if students should not see correct answers after submitting. Form questions do not update automatically when lesson questions change.
    Share only the Collect responses link. Keep collaboration, results-summary, and workbook sharing off for your personal collection. School administrators and school policies still apply to your work account. This website does not collect student passwords or expose your OneDrive sign-in to students.

    Forms and its Excel connection manage live responses. This website opens your results; it does not read workbook rows or automatically create Microsoft Forms. Your questions and links remain in saved lessons and backups.

    USE YOUR CHATGPT SUBSCRIPTION

    Textbook → ChatGPT → lesson

    Prepare your textbook pages here, work with them in ChatGPT, then bring the result back for review. Your school lesson-plan format stays unchanged.

    Guided ChatGPT workflow. This website does not call ChatGPT or use your subscription automatically. You attach the pages and request in ChatGPT yourself; its plan limits apply. No API key or extra API service is connected. Files stay in this browser until you choose to share or back them up.

    1Choose textbook pages

    Upload a PDF, page photos, or a text file. Select only the parts needed for this lesson. Up to 12 selected pages or photos; PDFs up to 50 MB, photos up to 8 MB each. PDF page numbers mean the file’s page order.

    Enter page numbers or ranges. Choose up to 12 pages in total.

    Digital PDFs can provide text. Scanned pages and photographs need ChatGPT to read the pictures you attach there. Check extracted text for errors; this website has no automatic photo text recognition. Selected page pictures and notes are kept with the lesson and its backups.

    2Prepare your ChatGPT request

    Lesson details, objectives, activity descriptions, questions and answers already entered here are reused. Student response/result links are excluded.

    Open ChatGPT ↗

    In ChatGPT, paste this request or attach the downloaded request file. Attach the textbook PDF and specify the selected pages, or download page pictures below and attach those. ChatGPT will be asked to create a downloadable lesson-draft file; you do not write any code.

    3Bring back and review your lesson

    Download the lesson-draft JSON file ChatGPT provides and choose it here. If ChatGPT gives a reply instead, copy and paste its complete reply below. This is separate from My lessons backup import.

    Review school-plan content

    These are proposed contents for your approved template, not a new layout. Missing school-specific information must stay marked “Not provided”. Reflection is completed after teaching.

    A separate lesson keeps the current lesson in My lessons first. Adding slides preserves its existing activities, pictures and approved versions. The new plan is a working draft; approve it separately after review.
